Holiday kitchen

Is a countertop oven worth it for holiday sides?

Work backward from serving time, the pans you already own, and what actually needs the main oven.

Updated 2026-11-20 · 3 min read · no product tested · no prices or ratings

Write the menu as a schedule

List the dishes you need hot at the same time. Put the largest pan on the list first, then write down the dish that always ends up waiting for oven space. That second dish is what you’re shopping for.

Use the pan, not the quart number

Measure your sheet pan rim to rim. If you’re comparing the listed Emeril Lagasse French-door model, the seller’s 26-quart figure describes cavity volume. It isn’t a promise that your pan fits. Check usable width and depth and the clearance in front of both doors.

Plan how you’ll check doneness

FoodSafety.gov lists 165°F for poultry and leftovers. Use a food thermometer to check the food itself. Oven presets and the appliance display don’t tell you its internal temperature.

If your second dish already fits the oven you own, save the counter space.
