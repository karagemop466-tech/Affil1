Holiday hosting: what should a
counter appliance do for you?

Between Thanksgiving and New Year's, the question stops being “which air fryer is best?” and becomes “what do I need help with at 4 p.m. on a Thursday?” A seasonal checklist for choosing — or using — a compact counter appliance this December.

Season note · Good Measure Kitchen · First published 20 November 2026

Start with the holiday problem, not the appliance

Oven space is the scarce resource in late December. Roast, sides, and dessert all want the same rack at the same time, so the practical question is: which one job would you happily move off the oven? Crisping a tray of vegetables, reheating plates in batches, keeping a second dish warm — name the job first. An appliance chosen around a real, recurring job is more likely to earn its counter spot than one chosen from a feature list.

Four questions to answer before you click “buy”

  1. What is the one job? Write down the dish or task you would move off the oven. If you cannot name one, the appliance may end up as seasonal clutter — a storage problem wearing a store badge.
  2. Where will it live during the weeks it matters? Measure the counter (or the cabinet it retreats to), including the maker's required clearance for ventilation and door swing. Check the official product dimensions and manual before buying — never a listing thumbnail.
  3. What does cleanup look like at 9 p.m.? Holiday cooking already fills the sink. Identify every removable, food-contact part in the official manual and what the maker says about washing them. Do not assume dishwasher safety.
  4. How many batches, realistically? Think in portions for your busiest meal, not in headline capacity numbers. A box figure does not tell you how your usual food fits; a usable-area measurement or the manual's arrangement guidance tells you more.

A quiet trick: the “half-batch rehearsal”

Before the big day, run the appliance once on an ordinary dinner. Time it, watch where it sits, wash its parts, put it away. Ten minutes of rehearsal answers more questions than any comparison table — and if the routine annoys you in week one, it will not survive week three of December.
