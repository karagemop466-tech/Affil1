Trade-offs

How should you weigh cleanup and counter space against each other?

Both costs are paid every week, and both are usually ignored in favour of features. Here is a way to price them before you buy.

By the shopnow047c-20 desk 5 min read Reviewed 2026-11-20 No hands-on testing claimed

Count the parts that touch food

A single-basket fryer is a basket plus a crisper tray. A dual-basket unit doubles that. A French-door oven adds racks, a crumb tray, and any accessories included.

Count the parts and picture washing all of them on a weeknight. That number is the honest cleaning cost, and it scales with the appliance, not with how much food you cooked.

Separate dishwasher-safe from hand-wash-only

Nonstick coatings degrade under scrubbing, so dishwasher-safe parts are a genuine longevity feature and not a convenience gimmick. Check the listing for which specific parts carry the rating, because it is often the basket and tray but not the housing or the accessories.

If nothing is dishwasher safe, budget for hand washing every time, and weight that heavily.

Price the counter space as a permanent cost

Counter space is not a one-time cost; it is paid every day. A unit left out is used; a unit put away is not. The fair comparison is not square inches but whether the appliance can stay out.

A useful test: mark the footprint with tape for a week before buying. If the tape annoys you, the appliance will too.

Weigh the two against each other, not in isolation

A big oven-style unit costs more counter and more cleaning but replaces a toaster oven, which nets out. A small basket unit costs almost no counter but adds a second appliance if the toaster stays.

The trade-off is only real when you count what gets removed from the counter, not just what gets added.

Decide which cost you will actually resent

People tolerate one of these badly. Some cannot stand a cluttered counter; others cannot stand scrubbing. Whichever one you resent, weight it double.

That is not a hedge; it is the most predictive single factor in whether the appliance gets used.

The short version

Count the parts, check which are dishwasher safe, and tape the footprint for a week. If the appliance cannot stay on the counter, the cleaning cost never gets paid because the appliance never gets used.
