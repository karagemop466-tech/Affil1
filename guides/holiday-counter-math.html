SEASONAL · HOLIDAY NOTES

Holiday-week counter math:
where does a second cooking zone fit?

The main oven is the star of the holiday week — and its bottleneck. A second countertop cooking zone can shorten the queue, but only if it has a measured home, a planned job and a cleanup path. Do the math before the week, not during it.

Editorial guide · Good Measure Kitchen · 20 November 2026 · 5 min read

The oven queue, written down

List every dish the main oven must handle on the day, with rough timings: the roast, the traybakes, the reheats, the finale. Most kitchens find one or two items that don't actually need the big oven — sides, reheats, crisp-finish jobs. Those are the second zone's job description. Write it down now; decide the appliance later.

The three measurements that save the day

  • The spot. Width, depth, height and — critically — door or drawer clearance. Tape-measure it, photograph it, and keep the photo on your phone for when you're standing in a listing page.
  • The circuit. Kitchens run out of outlets before they run out of counter. Confirm where the appliance plugs in and what shares that circuit — official wattage ratings are on the listing or rating plate.
  • The landing zone. Hot food needs a trivet-and-towel staging spot within one step. If that spot doesn't exist, the second zone creates traffic, not capacity.

The cleanup contract

Guests judge the meal; the household judges the sink. Before the week: identify every removable part of the second-zone appliance from its manual, check the official care instructions, and give each part a named spot in the dish rack rotation. A second zone that doubles the washing-up loses the room's goodwill by Friday. Our cleanup and counter-space checklist walks the routine.

A calm-week checklist

  1. Write the oven queue; underline items that don't need the main oven.
  2. Measure the candidate spot with clearances; photograph it.
  3. Check outlets and shared circuits.
  4. Choose the format that matches the job — then verify the exact listing's dimensions and care instructions.
  5. Name the landing zone and the dish-rack plan.
  6. Do one full practice run in the week before, end to end.
