How to assess cleanup and counter-space trade-offs

The ten minutes after dinner is the real test of a kitchen appliance.

The Good Measure DeskEditorial deskPublished 2026-11-20 · Updated 2026-11-20

Cooking is the part that gets photographed. Wash-up is the part you live with, every single evening, for as long as the appliance is in your kitchen.

Ask what actually goes in the dishwasher

Not the accessory count. The basket, the tray and the drip tray. A dishwasher-safe basket removes the worst part of the job; a removable drip tray removes the worst part of the smell problem. If the heating element is exposed and sits above the basket, expect the ceiling of the cavity to need a wipe.

Ask where the oil goes

Air frying uses a fraction of the oil of deep frying, which is a genuine advantage, but the fat still has to go somewhere. A sealed or channelised drip path into a removable tray is easier to live with than a flat floor that collects. This is one of the most visible differences between formats and it is rarely written down clearly.

Ask where it lives

Counter space you keep clear for an appliance you use daily is different from cupboard space you clear twice a year. If you are going to store it, check the weight and the depth of the cupboard. If you are going to leave it out, decide where it will sit and accept that it is now part of the kitchen's visual footprint.

Ask how it sounds and smells

A fan that runs loudly changes the experience of cooking every night, and a vent that pushes hot air across a wall changes the wall. Neither appears in a specification sheet, and both are the sort of thing readers write to us about.

Before you rely on this

What we would need to confirm: whether each basket, tray and drip component is listed as dishwasher-safe, and the internal clearances. Only what the listing states is repeated above — nothing is inferred.
