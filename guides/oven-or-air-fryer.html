Conventional oven or air fryer?
A fit check, not a verdict.

An air fryer can cost less to run for small batches — and more for big ones. The answer depends on what you cook, how often, and what your appliances are rated at.

Editorial guide · Good Measure Kitchen · 20 November 2026

Questions to work through

  1. What do you cook most often? Single portions and reheating tend to suit an air fryer; roasts, traybakes and multiple dishes at once tend to suit an oven.
  2. What is each appliance’s wattage? Find the rating plate or official listing for the exact model. Wattage × hours used × your electricity unit rate gives a rough running-cost comparison — not a bill prediction.
  3. How much food fits at once? Compare usable cooking area and rack layout, not headline capacity figures. Check the official manual for the exact model.
  4. Preheat time matters. Ovens usually need preheating; many air-fryer formats do not, or need far less. That can shrink the cost gap for short cooks.
  5. Where will it live? Measure counter, cabinet and ventilation clearances against the official dimensions before buying.
