Frozen food nights: four questions that decide whether a basket earns its counter

Most air-fryer purchases are justified on weeknights, not weekends. Before you buy one for the freezer aisle, four questions are worth answering with your own kitchen in front of you.

Updated 2026-11-20 · Crisp & Counter

Illustration made for this publication: not product photography, not an image from a listing, and not a depiction of the item a link leads to.

Before you start

The appliance you use twice a week matters more than the one that impresses guests once. For a lot of households the twice-a-week job is simple: something frozen, something that should end up hot in the middle and crisp outside, in about the time it takes to set the table.

Question 1: how many pieces, in one layer?

Frozen food is the clearest case of the single-layer rule. Nuggets, fries, wings, patties and fish fingers all release surface moisture; stacked, they steam each other and come out pale and leathery. So the useful number is not quarts, it is how many things fit on the floor. Count what your household actually eats in one sitting. If that exceeds what one layer holds, you are buying a machine you will run in batches — which is fine, as long as you knew before paying.

That listing describes an 8-quart basket and, unusually, puts “Frozen” in its own title as a named use. Note what that is and is not: it is a claim that a mode exists. It is not a time-and-temperature table, and it is not a promise about how your chicken tenders will turn out.

Question 2: does anything in the box get in the way?

Some baskets have a crisper plate that reduces usable floor. Some oven-style units have racks that let you run two levels for items that do not need contact — a real advantage for reheating pizza or roasted vegetables, a disadvantage for anything that needs a hot surface. Look at the accessory list, not the marketing line, and ask which shape your food is.

Question 3: can you clean it without a ritual?

On a weeknight, cleaning is the whole decision. A basket that goes in the dishwasher is a materially different object from one that needs soaking; nonstick that chips in a year is a different object from bare metal you can scrub. Where a listing says “Nonstick Dishwasher-Safe Basket”, that is the seller describing the parts; anything beyond that — how long the coating survives your sponge — is a question for owner reviews on the retailer's own page, which we deliberately do not reproduce here.

Question 4: where does it sit, permanently?

A frozen-food appliance that lives in a cupboard is not a frozen-food appliance. Measure the piece of counter you are genuinely willing to surrender, including the arc of a door that opens downward and the few centimetres of rear clearance a fan needs. Then check the listing's stated footprint, or ask the manufacturer for it — we leave that field blank rather than guessing, and we say why on the flagged register.

Before buying anything, run one frozen-food dinner in a conventional oven at a high temperature on a preheated sheet pan and time it honestly, including cleaning. If that was already fine, an air fryer is a convenience upgrade, not a solution — and you should buy it on those terms, with expectations that match.

What we are not telling you

Nothing here is a cooked result. We have not measured browning, texture, noise, energy use or timing in any machine. Capability wording is quoted from listings and attributed as such. Prices and ratings are absent by policy, not by oversight.
