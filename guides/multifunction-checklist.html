Questions to ask before buying a multi-function kitchen appliance

Nine programs is a feature. Four that you will actually use is a purchase.

The Good Measure DeskEditorial deskPublished 2026-11-20 · Updated 2026-11-20

Multi-function appliances are sold on the length of the mode list. That is the easiest number for a manufacturer to make large and the least useful number for a buyer to make small.

Write your four before you look at any listing

Sit down for three minutes and write the four cooking jobs you actually want the appliance to do. Air fry. Roast. Toast. Dehydrate. Bake. Reheat. The list is usually shorter than you expect, and it is the same list you will use every week.

Separate the modes from the presets

A mode lets you set a temperature and a time. A preset is a one-touch combination of both that Amazon or the brand decided for you. Presets are a convenience when they match your food and a nuisance when they do not, because you end up overriding them anyway. Count the modes, and treat the presets as a bonus.

Check the manual is actually readable

A multi-function appliance is only as good as its documentation. Before you buy, find the manual and look at the cooking charts. If the charts are thin, you will spend your first month guessing.

Check what it needs beside it

Oven formats often need a trivet, a drip tray and a specific rack position per mode. Accessories that are “included” may be six pieces that are mostly interchangeable wire racks. Count what you would have to buy separately.

Check the failure modes

Every multi-function appliance has one thing it does badly. For an oven format it is usually toast and small items near the element. For a basket format it is anything wet that drips onto the element. Use the appliance for two weeks before you decide it is a bargain.
