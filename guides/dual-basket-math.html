Dual basket or two separate machines? The counter math nobody writes down

Two baskets in one box looks obviously better than one. The comparison that matters is against two small single-basket machines — on outlets, on cleanup, on cooking floor, and on what happens when one half breaks.

Before you start

A dual-basket machine is marketed as two appliances in one. Judge it against the obvious alternative: two of the cheapest decent single-basket machines, on the same counter, for the same money or less.

Where two machines win

The questions no comparison table answers

Buy the dual basket if your recurring problem is “two different things must finish at the same time”. Buy one larger single basket if your recurring problem is “there is too much of one thing”. Buy two cheap machines only if you have the counter space, two outlets on different circuits, and no appetite for a coordinated appliance.
