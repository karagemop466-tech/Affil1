CHOOSING A STYLE · BUYER’S NOTEBOOK

Basket or oven-style
air fryer?

A useful comparison starts with your counter and your routine—not a feature-count contest.

Editorial guide · Good Measure Kitchen · 20 November 2026

First, picture a normal weeknight

What do you make most often? How many portions do you want ready at once? Do you prefer a compact single-purpose appliance, or do you expect to use more than one cooking function? Those are questions to answer for your own household; this guide does not assert that either design is universally better.

Compare these six things

  1. Counter footprint: measure the intended spot, including room to operate and ventilate as the manufacturer directs. Check the official product dimensions and manual before buying.
  2. Usable cooking area: compare basket shape or rack layout—not just a headline capacity figure. Consider the food you actually cook and whether it can sit in a single layer when needed.
  3. Batch routine: would you rather cook one batch and shake/turn it, or arrange food across shelves and manage their positions? Verify the operating instructions for the exact model.
  4. Functions you will use: list the functions you truly need. Extra modes are not automatically extra value if they complicate setup or storage.
  5. Cleanup: inspect which parts are removable and what the official manual says about washing them. Never assume dishwasher safety.
  6. Storage and access: consider appliance weight, cabinet height, cord placement, door clearance and where accessories will live.

A small decision exercise

Give each factor a personal importance score from 1 to 3, then compare only products with verified specifications. If a model’s dimension or accessory details are not confirmed from an official listing or manual, leave that comparison blank. A blank is more useful than a guess.
