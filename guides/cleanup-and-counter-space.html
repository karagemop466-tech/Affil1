How to assess cleanup and counter-space trade-offs

The appliance that gets used is the one that is easy to put away and easy to wash. Both of those are checkable before you buy.

Updated 2026-11-20 · 5 min read · no product tested · no prices or ratings

Count the parts before you count the features

An air fryer's real cost is paid every time you use it, in washing and in putting it away. That cost is set by how many separate pieces come out and how big they are.

A single basket is usually a basket and a crisper plate. A dual-basket machine doubles that. An oven typically adds a rack, a tray, and sometimes a rotisserie or a basket accessory. None of those are bad — but you wash them, and you store them, and one of them is always slightly too big for the sink.

The four questions that predict whether you will keep using it

Does it fit in your sink?
Take the internal width and depth of the appliance's largest removable part and compare it with your sink. A tray that does not lie flat gets washed at an angle and gets washed badly.
Where does it live when it is not in use?
If the answer is 'on the counter', it needs to earn that space permanently. If the answer is 'in a cupboard', check the weight and whether the cupboard is above shoulder height.
What has to move to make room?
The honest test is not whether it fits, but what leaves the counter to let it in. If you cannot name the thing that moves, you have not made room, you have just added.
How long is the cord, and where is the socket?
A cord that does not reach means an extension lead across a work surface, which is both a nuisance and something the manufacturer's own instructions may warn against.

Dishwasher-safe is a claim, not a fact you own

Listings often say a basket is dishwasher-safe. Whether that applies to your machine, your detergent and your cycle is a question for the manufacturer's own instructions for the exact model — and it is worth checking, because a non-stick coating that is ruined by a hot cycle is an expensive way to learn this.

Where this site cannot confirm a care instruction from an official manual, it says nothing rather than saying something plausible.

A five-minute layout test

Before ordering, cut a rectangle of paper to the appliance's published width and depth, put it where the machine would go, and then try to use the kitchen around it for a day. Open the cupboard the paper is blocking. Stand where you normally stand.

It sounds excessive. It is also the cheapest way to discover that the thing you were about to buy makes the most-used part of your counter unusable, and it costs one sheet of paper.
