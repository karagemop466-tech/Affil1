How to assess cleanup and counter-space trade-offs

The parts you wash and the space it occupies decide whether you keep using it. Both are checkable in about five minutes.

Updated 2026-11-20 · Crisp & Counter

Illustration. Not a photograph of any product sold here.

Count the parts before the features

An air fryer's real cost is paid every time you use it, in washing and putting away. That cost is set by how many separate pieces come out and how big they are.

A single basket is usually a basket and a crisper plate. A dual-basket machine doubles that. An oven typically adds a rack, a tray and sometimes a basket accessory. None of that is bad — but you wash it, you store it, and one piece is always slightly too big for the sink.

Four questions that predict whether you keep using it

Does the largest part fit in your sink?
Measure the removable part, not the machine. A tray that has to be washed at an angle gets washed badly.
Where does it live when it is cold?
On the counter means it must earn permanent space. In a cupboard means checking the weight and whether the shelf is above shoulder height.
What moves out to let it in?
The honest test is not whether it fits, but what leaves the counter. If you cannot name the thing, you have not made room, you have added.
How long is the cord, and where is the socket?
A cord that does not reach means an extension lead across a work surface, which the manufacturer's own instructions may warn against.

Dishwasher-safe is a claim, not a fact you own

Listings often say a basket is dishwasher-safe. Whether that applies to your machine, your detergent and your cycle is a question for the manufacturer's instructions for that exact model. A coating ruined by a hot cycle is an expensive way to learn this.

Where a care instruction could not be confirmed from an official manual, this site says nothing rather than something plausible.

The five-minute layout test

Before ordering, cut a rectangle of paper to the published width and depth, put it where the machine would go, and use the kitchen around it for a day. Open the cupboard the paper blocks. Stand where you normally stand.

It sounds excessive. It is also the cheapest way to find out that the thing you were about to buy makes the most-used part of the counter unusable — and it costs one sheet of paper.

The trade in one line

More capacity means more surface to wash and more counter to give up. The right answer is the smallest machine whose parts you will still be willing to wash in February.
