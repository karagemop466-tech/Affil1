A no-hype checklist for comparing air fryer capacity

Quart numbers measure total air volume, including empty headroom above the food. Browning depends on usable floor area and keeping pieces in a single breathable layer.

Prompt Core Hypothesis #2 — working editorial hypothesis, not a popularity or lab-test claim

Why 6 quarts in two drawers is not three-quarters of an 8-quart basket

Volume labels look interchangeable until you try to lay down four salmon fillets or a wide flatbread. On P-01, the seller title specifies 6 quarts as two independent 3-quart frying baskets. Dividing volume across a center wall gives you two separate zones, each with the footprint of a compact 3-quart basket.

On P-03, the seller title specifies a single 8-quart XL basket. Without a divider wall, longer cuts of fish, wider batches of wings, or a round cake pan have continuous floor width — at the cost of running everything at one temperature.

Why a 26-quart toaster oven plays by rack-spacing rules

P-02 lists a 26-quart interior in its seller title. Much of a toaster oven's cubic volume comes from vertical height across rack positions. When air-frying on multiple racks at once, top and bottom trays do not always receive identical airflow unless rotated.

Count capacity by how many portions lie flat without overlapping, not by how much food can be piled vertically to the heating element.

Four capacity questions to check on the official listing before buying

Because interior basket length, width, and rack clearance are not stated in the listing titles verified in this pass, leave those measurements unassumed and verify four specifics on the product page or brand manual: (1) interior floor length × width per basket, (2) height from crisper plate to upper guard, (3) whether two racks can cook simultaneously without blocking airflow, and (4) dishwasher rack fit in your own sink or machine.
