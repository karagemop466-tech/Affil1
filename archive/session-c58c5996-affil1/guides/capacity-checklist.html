A no-hype checklist
for capacity.

A capacity number is only one clue. Match the actual cooking area to a meal you make often.

Editorial guide · Good Measure Kitchen · 20 November 2026

Try this practical check

  1. Write down the usual food and number of portions you prepare.
  2. Check the official product listing for the exact model’s stated capacity, dimensions and included parts.
  3. Look for a usable-area measurement or illustration in the official manual; a volume alone may not describe how food fits.
  4. Ask whether the food needs space around it or must be arranged in a single layer, and consult the maker’s instructions.
  5. Measure your counter, cabinet and storage location. Allow for the clearances specified by the manufacturer.
  6. Check whether your usual meal requires one batch or several; do not infer cooking time or results from capacity.

Make a shortlist that fits

Use a simple table for your own needs: target portions, available footprint, cooking area, storage, cleaning requirements and source. Add a source and checked date beside every product-specific value. If the source is missing, leave the cell blank rather than turning a guess into a recommendation.

Questions worth asking yourself

  • Which meal do I want this appliance to make easier?
  • How much counter space can I leave available?
  • Would the appliance be easy to lift, store and clean in my kitchen?
  • What compromises am I willing to make between compactness and batch size?

These prompts are intended to support personal comparison. They do not establish a universal capacity threshold or recommend a specific model.
