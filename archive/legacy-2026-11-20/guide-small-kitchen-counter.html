Small kitchen, big counter question: where does it actually live?

Measure the strip, the clearance and the retreat — before you buy, not after.

Working title — not a claim about popularity, search volume or product superiority. Good Measure Kitchen Notes · First published 20 November 2026

The counter is the spec sheet

In a small kitchen, the most important specification is not wattage or preset count. It is the strip of counter where the appliance will live — and whether it can retreat when it is not working. A machine that fits on paper but blocks the sink, the kettle or the door swing will lose every week, no matter how good its features are.

Measure three things, in this order

  1. The strip. Measure width, depth and height of the exact spot, including the maker's required clearance for ventilation. Oven-style models need door swing; basket models need drawer pull-out. Check official dimensions and the manual — never a listing thumbnail.
  2. The retreat. Where does it go when it is not cooking? A cabinet needs its own measurements, including height with the door closed. If there is no retreat, the appliance lives on the counter permanently, and that is a design decision, not an accident.
  3. The cord. Where is the nearest outlet, and does the cord reach without crossing the sink or hob? A cord that crosses a wet zone is a daily annoyance and a safety question. Measure the distance; do not guess it.

Shape notes for tight counters

  • Dual-basket is the widest basket shape. Two baskets side by side need width you may not have. Measure the full width, not one basket.
  • Oven-style / French door is the deepest. Door swing and counter depth decide whether it works. The 26-quart cavity is generous; the footprint is the price.
  • Single-basket is usually the smallest footprint of the three. If the strip is narrow, this shape starts with an advantage — but batch math still applies (see the capacity checklist).

The one-week counter test

Before buying, mark the footprint on the counter with tape for a week. Cook normally around it. If you start moving it to reach things, you have your answer. Tape is cheaper than a return.
