The living-with-it question

How to assess cleanup and counter-space trade-offs

The appliance you resent is the one that does not fit. Six questions that decide whether a machine earns a permanent place on your counter.

Updated 20 November 2026·4 min·The Pantry Journal

Measure the space the machine needs, not the space it occupies

Every air fryer needs more room than its own footprint. A drawer needs clear space in front of it to pull out, plus room for your hand. A door needs its swing. A machine under a wall cabinet needs clearance above for the lid, the handle or the exhaust.

The mistake is measuring the gap on the counter and not the volume around it. Take the tape measure to the actual place the machine will live, and measure the path it has to travel in and out of the cupboard if that is where it will go.

Then measure the sink

The part that gets washed is the part that decides whether you use the machine on a Tuesday. A large single basket is easy to clean and awkward to fit in a small sink. An oven's shelves and cavity need wiping in place, which is less work per use but harder to do properly.

If the removable parts do not fit flat in your sink, you will end up cleaning them at an angle, badly.

Six questions, in order

Run these against any machine you are considering. They take two minutes and eliminate most of them.

  • Does the removable part fit flat in my sink?
  • Is there clear space in front of the machine for a drawer or a door?
  • Is there clearance above it for the lid, handle or vents?
  • Can I reach the back of the interior without pulling the machine out?
  • Are the surfaces that get dirty removable, or do they have to be wiped in place?
  • Where does the cord go, and is there a socket that is not behind the machine?

The counter-space maths nobody does

An appliance on the counter is a permanent rent payment on your kitchen. If it saves you ten minutes a week but occupies the only workspace you have, the trade is bad regardless of how well it cooks.

The honest test: would you still want it if it never went in a cupboard? If the answer is no, the machine will end up in the cupboard, and then it will stop being used. That is not a product failure. It is a sizing failure.

Questions people ask

Are dishwasher-safe baskets actually dishwasher-safe?

The listings in our set claim dishwasher-safe baskets. We reproduce the claim and label it as the seller's. Whether the coating survives years of dishwasher cycles is exactly the kind of thing a listing will not tell you.

Is an oven-style air fryer harder to clean than a basket?

Different, rather than harder. A basket is one part you can soak. An oven is a cavity you wipe, plus shelves and door glass. Which one you find more annoying depends on your sink and your patience.

Does a bigger machine take longer to clean?

Usually more surface, but the number of parts matters more than the size. Count the parts that touch food before you count the quarts.

What this guide will not do

It will not tell you which machine is best. Nobody here has tested one, and the FTC's guidance is clear that an endorsement has to reflect an honest experience. What the guide does is narrow the field using the questions a listing will not answer for you.
