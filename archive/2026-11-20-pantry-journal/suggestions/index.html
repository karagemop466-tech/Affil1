Review queue

Every change, before it ships

This is the page you asked for: a list you can review and choose from before anything is implemented. Each entry says what it would change, why, what it costs, and what would have to be true before it is safe.

Read the shortlist at the top. Each item has an id. If you want one implemented, note the id on the Suggestions page in the site and it becomes a task with a named scope.

If you only read five

  1. #1 · SUG-21 — Unblocks the only measurement that matters: whether a click is ever attributed.
  2. #2 · SUG-22 — The one number a buyer wants and we are not allowed to publish yet; this is how it becomes publishable.
  3. #3 · SUG-20 — Seventeen pages compete for one host; consolidating is the highest-leverage SEO change available.
  4. #4 · SUG-24 — Turns 'we have no data' into a first-party number, without cookies.
  5. #5 · SUG-25 — Testimonials are the difference between a listing and a shop; this is the compliant route to getting them.

Shipped Lead every product page with a plain-English verdict, then the claims SUG-01

Impact 5/5 Effort 2/5 Confidence: Hypothesis with strong face validity

A buyer decides in the first screen. A page that opens with a register of identifiers gives them nothing to decide with. Each product page now opens with a quick take written from the listing's own words, then a labelled claims table.

Done: Live: the five product pages in shop/products/.

Evidence needed: None to ship. Time-on-page and outbound clicks would confirm it.

Shipped Put the disclosure in a strip under the masthead on every page, and again in the footer SUG-02

Impact 5/5 Effort 1/5 Confidence: High

The FTC's test is whether a reader can weigh the recommendation at the moment they read it. A footer-only disclosure fails that on a long page. Amazon's Agreement section 5 requires the statement to be clear and prominent.

Done: Generated by one template and now enforced by tools/shop_check.py, which fails the build if the strip is missing or appears after the first outbound product link.

Evidence needed: None. Sources are quoted on the Sources page.

Shipped Publish no price, rating, review count or availability figure anywhere SUG-03

Impact 5/5 Effort 1/5 Confidence: High.

Program Policies 2(b) permits price and availability display only where Amazon serves the link carrying the data, or where it arrives through the Creators API or PA API. No feed is connected. A stale price is also a misrepresentation risk under the same section.

Done: Every product page states what is excluded and why, in a block directly under the buy button, so the absence reads as a decision rather than a gap.

Evidence needed: None.

Shipped Keep the submitted code and the observed ASIN in separate fields, and say so in public SUG-04

Impact 5/5 Effort 1/5 Confidence: High

The submitted code is a 9-character short-link slug; the ASIN is the 10-character code after /dp/. Merging them destroys the audit trail that would catch a wrong-product redirect.

Done: Product pages show the observed ASIN; a table of record shows the submitted code beside it, labelled, with the observation date.

Shipped Link to direct tagged amazon.com/dp/ URLs, not through the supplied short links SUG-05

Impact 5/5 Effort 2/5 Confidence: High on the policy text

Program Policies 2(b) requires the Associates tag as a URL parameter on each link, and the Agreement prohibits anything that obscures the Amazon destination. Direct /dp/ URLs with ?tag= satisfy both conditions visibly. This does not assert that the supplied links are invalid — their status remains unresolved on the Flagged page.

Done: Every buy button uses a direct /dp/ URL carrying ?tag=shopnow047c-20 and rel="sponsored nofollow noopener". Each page also lists the supplied short link for transparency.

Evidence needed: One-link test in the Associates dashboard (see SUG-21).

Shipped Build a publication front page instead of a dashboard SUG-06

Impact 5/5 Effort 3/5 Confidence: Hypothesis.

A dashboard tells the reader about the seller's process. A front page tells the reader what the shop sells and why it is worth two minutes. Naming, typography, photography and section order now read as a shop.

Done: Home page leads with a hero, five product cards, editorial sections, then the method.

Shipped Organise the shop by the two categories buyers actually distinguish SUG-07

Impact 4/5 Effort 2/5 Confidence: Hypothesis.

Air fryers by shape (basket, dual basket, oven) and thermometers by job (instant-read, wireless leave-in) match how people describe what they want. 'Category' pages in the old sections were by appliance type only and ignored the thermometers entirely.

Done: shop/categories/ has an Air fryers and a Thermometers page, both linked from the main navigation and both in the sitemap.

Shipped Publish the three question-shaped guides with answerable FAQs SUG-08

Impact 4/5 Effort 3/5 Confidence: Hypothesis

Question-shaped pages can earn a featured answer and give a reader a reason to arrive. Each guide ends with an FAQ whose answers are either sourced or explicitly framed as advice.

Done: Three guides live; two more scheduled on the editorial calendar and published as drafts with honest notes, not filler.

Shipped Give every page its own canonical URL, structured data, sitemap and feed SUG-09

Impact 4/5 Effort 3/5 Confidence: High on mechanics

One indexable site needs one set of URLs, one sitemap and a feed that search engines can subscribe to. Structured data is what lets a result carry a headline and a breadcrumb rather than a bare link.

Done: Per-page canonical, BreadcrumbList, Article and ItemList JSON-LD, plus FAQPage on guides. sitemap.xml, feed.xml and robots.txt are generated by the build.

Shipped Replace the old register pages with one buyer-readable table of record, and keep the audit language behind it SUG-10

Impact 4/5 Effort 2/5 Confidence: High

A reader wants to know which machine does what. An auditor wants to know what was observed and when. Both are legitimate, so the shop splits them: product pages for the buyer, one Table of record for the audit.

Done: shop/table/ holds the full observation register with source links; product pages cite it rather than repeat it.

Shipped Use editorial photography that is clearly not the product, and say so SUG-11

Impact 3/5 Effort 2/5 Confidence: High on the compliance reason

Amazon's IP licence restricts use of Amazon-served imagery, and misrepresenting a stock photo as the product would be a misleading claim. Food photography sets a mood; a caption under each image says what it is.

Done: Three generated editorial images, each captioned as an illustration with no product shown.

Shipped Write a plain-language About page that admits the AI assistance SUG-12

Impact 3/5 Effort 1/5 Confidence: High on the trust argument.

Readers forgive a lot; they do not forgive being misled about who wrote the page. The About page names the account holder, the role of AI assistance and the sourcing standard.

Done: shop/about/.

Shipped Cut the navigation to one group of five labels SUG-13

Impact 3/5 Effort 1/5 Confidence: High.

The previous pages spread fifteen destinations across every section, which reads as an internal tool. Five labels — Shop, Categories, Guides, About, Suggestions — cover every reader need. Everything else lives in the footer.

Done: One navigation group sitewide; the full site map is one click away in the footer.

Shipped Keep every dashboard surface reachable from the footer SUG-14

Impact 2/5 Effort 1/5 Confidence: High.

The brief asked for the dashboards to stay reachable while the front page became a publication. That means footer links, not primary navigation.

Done: Footer carries the Table of record, the Audit trail, the Site map and links to the older sections that still exist in this repository.

Shipped Make the disclosure position and link markup machine-checked, not remembered SUG-15

Impact 4/5 Effort 2/5 Confidence: High.

A rule that depends on a person remembering it will eventually break, usually on the page that matters. Two checks now run on every build and in CI.

Done: tools/shop_check.py: disclosure before first outbound link on every page, every Amazon link carries rel sponsored + tag, every local link resolves, every page has a canonical and a title, XML parses.

Needs your decision Retire the two older sections, or keep them as archives SUG-16

Impact 5/5 Effort 2/5 Confidence: High on the SEO cost of duplicate topics

The repository currently publishes three overlapping sites from one host: the root storefront, crisp/ and shop/. That splits link equity and duplicates topics. The shop now covers the same ground better, so the choice is whether to redirect the old pages into the shop or freeze them with a note.

Evidence needed: Search Console coverage report after the first crawl would show which pages are competing.

  • Redirect: replace the old entry pages with a small page that links into the equivalent shop page, and drop them from the sitemaps.
  • Archive: leave the pages live, mark them as historical, and keep them out of the sitemap.
  • Do nothing: keep all three live and accept the split.

Blocked by: Decision. The files belong to other sessions' scopes, so this session will not edit them.

Needs your decision Decide whether to publish outbound-click counts as social proof SUG-17

Impact 3/5 Effort 1/5 Confidence: Hypothesis either way.

A shop that can say '412 readers went to this listing last month' feels alive. It is also a claim about traffic, and if the number is small it works against you. This needs a decision before any collector is built.

Evidence needed: A few weeks of real numbers to see whether they flatter or embarrass the shop.

Blocked by: Decision.

Needs your decision Choose a reader-capture provider, or decide not to capture at all SUG-18

Impact 4/5 Effort 3/5 Confidence: High that a list is valuable

An email list is the only audience asset that survives an algorithm change. It also creates a privacy obligation, a consent banner and a data processor. Doing it badly is worse than not doing it.

  • No capture for now: keep the site entirely cookieless and say so on the Privacy page.
  • A privacy-first hosted provider with a double opt-in and a short privacy notice.
  • Self-hosted collector plus a newsletter tool once there is enough traffic to justify the upkeep.

Blocked by: Decision plus an account.

Blocked on account Submit the shop sitemap in Search Console and confirm indexing SUG-19

Impact 5/5 Effort 1/5 Confidence: High.

Being findable is not the same as being indexed. Nothing on this site can verify that Google has crawled it; only Search Console can show coverage, queries and errors.

Evidence needed: A verified property and a sitemap submission under the account.

Blocked by: Account access to Search Console for this host.

Proposed Consolidate the host to one canonical set of URLs SUG-20

Impact 5/5 Effort 2/5 Confidence: High on the mechanism.

Three sections, thirty-plus pages and two sitemap sets currently share one origin. Google will pick its own canonical if the site does not state one clearly. The shop declares its own; the older pages do not point at it.

Evidence needed: None for the first step: add a canonical or a redirect from the older entry pages into the shop.

Blocked by: Scopes owned by other sessions; logged as a request rather than edited.

Blocked on account Run the one-link test in the Associates dashboard before promoting anything SUG-21

Impact 5/5 Effort 1/5 Confidence: High.

Every link on this site points at a direct /dp/ URL carrying ?tag=shopnow047c-20. Nobody has yet confirmed that a click on that URL appears in the tracking report. Until one click is confirmed, the whole shop is an unverified channel.

Evidence needed: One click, signed in, and the click appearing under shopnow047c-20 with a date.

Blocked by: Account access.

Blocked on account Adopt the Creators API to publish live prices lawfully SUG-22

Impact 5/5 Effort 4/5 Confidence: High on the policy route

The single most-wanted piece of information on a product page is the price. Program Policies 2(b) allows publishing it if it arrives through the Creators API or PA API. That turns the biggest gap on this site into a feature.

Evidence needed: API credentials and a build step that refreshes prices, plus a display that is truthful about freshness.

Blocked by: Account approval for the API.

Blocked on testing Buy, test and photograph the five appliances SUG-23

Impact 5/5 Effort 4/5 Confidence: High, and the FTC's guidance on endorsements requires an honest experience to endorse anyway.

The gap between this site and a shop people bookmark is first-hand experience. Testing lets the pages state what a machine is like to live with, which is the one thing a listing cannot tell you and an AI cannot invent.

Evidence needed: One unit per product, a method, and a dated record per test.

Blocked by: No units available to this session.

Proposed Deploy a first-party, cookieless click counter SUG-24

Impact 4/5 Effort 3/5 Confidence: High on feasibility, neutral on outcome.

The shop needs to know which product cards earn clicks before it can improve them. A counter that stores only 'page X, product Y, day Z, count' answers that without identifying anyone.

Evidence needed: A host that can run a small process. GitHub Pages cannot.

The site already sends the event if an endpoint is configured; with none configured, nothing is transmitted.

Blocked on testing Collect a handful of real reader testimonials, with permission SUG-25

Impact 4/5 Effort 3/5 Confidence: High, but only if the testimonials are real.

One sentence from a real buyer, used with permission and disclosed, does more than a page of method description. It also has to be genuine, which is why this cannot be written here.

Evidence needed: Written permission from each buyer and a record of what they actually said.

Blocked by: No buyers yet.

Rejected Publish star ratings and review counts scraped from the listing SUG-26

Impact 4/5 Effort 1/5 Confidence: High on the compliance risk.

Rejected. Program Policies 2(b) and section 6(a) restrict displaying and altering Program Content, and scraped numbers go stale silently. A stale rating is a misleading claim about a product.

Rejected on policy grounds, not on taste.

Rejected Add countdown timers and 'today only' deal badges SUG-27

Impact 3/5 Effort 1/5 Confidence: High.

Rejected. Program Policies 2(b) requires removing links and references to a limited-time promotion as soon as the promotion ends. A countdown creates a permanent removal obligation and a false urgency claim when it is wrong.

Rejected on policy grounds.

How to choose

Pick by the thing that is currently costing you money or credibility, not by the size of the idea. Right now that is SUG-21: if a click is never attributed, every other improvement is a nicer way to earn nothing. The five selected above are ranked on that basis, and the ranking is an opinion rather than a measurement.
