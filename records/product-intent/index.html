Product intent record

For every submitted link: the ASIN and listing this shop intended to promote, and — in a separate field — the destination that was actually observed. The two are never merged, because an observation is evidence of behaviour and intent is a statement by a person.

Why two fields. Amazon identifies a product by a 10-character ASIN, which makes intent checkable. If a redirect is written into the intent field, the record stops being checkable — it just restates what the link did, and a wrong redirect becomes indistinguishable from a correct decision. So intended_asin stays empty until the person who submitted the link says which product they meant, or until an Associates report shows it.

Field rules

These rules are part of the record, not documentation about it:

FieldRule
intended_asinSet only from a statement by the link submitter or from an Associates tracking report. Never inferred from a redirect. Null when unknown.
official_listing_urlCanonical https://www.amazon.com/dp/<ASIN> built from intended_asin only. Null when intended_asin is null.
confirmation_dateISO date the intent was confirmed. Null when unconfirmed.
confirmation_sourceWhat confirmed it (person, message, report id). Null when unconfirmed.
observed_redirect_targetExact URL the short link resolved to during automated observation. Evidence only. Never copied into intended_asin.
observed_atISO timestamp of the observation.
observation_methodHow the observation was made, so it can be repeated.
statusone of: confirmed | observed_only | unresolved
