Size it honestly

A no-hype checklist for comparing air fryer capacity

The quart number on the box is the least useful number on the box. Here is what to compare instead, and why two machines with the same figure can hold very different dinners.

Crisp & Counter editorial· 5 min read· Updated 2026-11-20· Affiliate links, disclosed above

Capacity is the number people compare and the number that misleads them most. A quart figure describes a volume. Dinner is a flat layer of food with air moving over it. Those are not the same thing, and no listing is obliged to reconcile them.

Start with what the number is describing

On a single-basket machine, the quart figure usually refers to the basket. On a dual-basket machine, it commonly refers to the total across both drawers — which means each drawer is roughly half. On an oven-style machine, it refers to the cavity, which includes space above the food that a basket does not have.

So “6 quart” on a dual basket and “6 quart” on a single basket are not the same claim about how much food fits at once. One of them can only cook one temperature at a time.

The arithmetic worth doing

A listing that says six quarts across two baskets is describing two baskets of about three quarts each. That is a different machine from one six-quart basket, and it is a different dinner. Read the listing title until you find the per-basket figure; if it is not there, ask before you buy.

The checklist, in order

  1. Is the figure per basket, or the total? If the title says two baskets plus one total, divide.
  2. Is it a basket volume or a cavity volume? Oven-style cavities include headroom a basket does not.
  3. What is the usable flat area? This is the number that predicts whether four chicken thighs fit in one layer. It is rarely published; look at the photos for the basket floor and judge proportion.
  4. How many layers does the design allow? A single drawer means one good layer. Racks in an oven-style machine may mean two, at the cost of airflow between them.
  5. Does the food you cook most often fit without stacking? Stacking is what turns a crisp result into a steamed one.
  6. Does a larger capacity still fit the counter? Every quart is paid for in footprint.

The question nobody asks: how much of the capacity is headroom?

Air fryers need space above the food for air to move. A basket filled to the rim is a basket that cooks unevenly, whatever its volume. This is why a stated capacity is best read as an upper bound on the cavity, not a target fill level. The practical capacity is smaller, and how much smaller depends on what you are cooking.

What this guide does not claim

It does not state a usable-capacity figure for any product, because none is published in a form we could confirm, and we did not measure any of them. It does not claim a larger machine is better. It does not rank the products by capacity, because the three capacities are describing three different kinds of space.
