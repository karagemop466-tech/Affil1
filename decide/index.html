Decision gate

What has to be known
before anything is decided

13 decisions turn this project. 3 can be made today from published sources, 2 are half answered and 8 are open — each one naming the artefact, dashboard or person that would close it, what finding out costs, and what the site ships meanwhile.

Nothing here is a forecast · every “what we have” is sourced · no value is filled in by inference

“We do not know yet” is only useful if it says what would tell us. Each row below separates three things that are usually blurred together: what is already established and where it came from, what is genuinely missing, and the concrete step that would retrieve it.

Read the who can settle it column first. 11 of the 13 decisions need an Associates dashboard this project cannot sign in to, and no amount of work inside the repository will close them. The rest are answerable from published sources or from a decision the owner simply has not made yet.

This page complements the question register rather than repeating it: that page lists what is known and missing per question; this one adds who, cost and default per decision.

The decisions that gate the others

Until D-03 is answered, any work that assumes a click is paid is guesswork, and everything downstream inherits that uncertainty.

D-01Decidable now

Should the site show prices, star ratings and review counts?

What we already have
Program Policies §2(b) permits price and availability only where Amazon serves the link carrying that data, or where it arrives through the Creators API or PA API. Amazon's own documentation now records PA-API 5 as deprecated and returning 403, and lists the Creators API prerequisites as Associates enrolment plus at least 10 qualifying sales within the past 30 days. A separate clause in the same section requires that a comparison-format price display also show the lowest used price.
What we do not have
Nothing that would change the answer. This one is decidable today.
How to find it out
Nothing to find out. The rule is published and was read directly on 2026-11-20.
Who can do that
Answered from official sources
Cost of finding out
Zero

What the site ships meanwhile

No prices, no ratings, no review counts. The comparison table keeps its blank columns and says why.

D-02Open

Are the five supplied links the products the owner meant to promote?

What we already have
All five links were opened on 2026-11-20 and each reached a live listing: B0CDY277D3, B09B7SB46R, B08DKYBTPH, B0DHZTCNR5, B0DZFD6R9Z. The listing titles and category breadcrumbs were transcribed on arrival.
What we do not have
The owner's intended ASIN for each link. Every submitted path code is 9 characters and contains lowercase letters; every ASIN on arrival is 10. That is evidence the codes are short-link slugs rather than identifiers, so intent cannot be read off the link, and filling intended_asin with the slug would be an inference.
How to find it out
For each product, open SiteStripe in the Associates dashboard, copy the tagged link for the exact ASIN intended, and record the intended ASIN beside the observed one. About five minutes per product.
Who can do that
Account owner only — SiteStripe requires sign-in
Cost of finding out
About 25 minutes of dashboard work

What the site ships meanwhile

The site describes the product each link actually reaches, labels the ASIN as observed rather than intended, and leaves the intended column null.

D-03Open

Does a click on these links earn anything?

What we already have
The tracking ID shopnow047c-20 was supplied. Program Policies §2(b) requires the Associates ID to appear as a URL parameter on each link, in the form XXXXX-##. The links were opened on 2026-11-20 and the arrival URLs carried lv, btn_type, gaOptInStatus, btn_ref, channelId and plpRedirect parameters. No tag parameter was observed.
What we do not have
One tracked click in the Associates click report. Without it the site cannot say whether the short-link service applies the tag server-side after the arrival page, or does not apply one at all. Both remain possible; neither has been shown.
How to find it out
Click one link from the owner's own device, then open the Associates click report for that day and look for the click. That report is the only permitted source for this number. Request sub-tag Associate IDs in the same session so later attribution can be read per page rather than in aggregate.
Who can do that
Account owner only
Cost of finding out
One click and about 20 minutes in the dashboard

What the site ships meanwhile

The site says attribution is unconfirmed, on the disclosure strip and on every product record. No page claims a click will pay.

D-04Decidable now

Which product is P-04?

What we already have
On 2026-11-20 the link link.amazon/B0c8moW59 reached amazon.com/dp/B0DHZTCNR5, a ThermoMaven instant-read thermometer, and the title was transcribed. An earlier session recorded three attempts that returned only Amazon's continuity page, and a parallel session briefly recorded the reverse mapping for P-04 and P-05 before a later fetch agreed with the register.
What we do not have
Nothing about identity — the register now agrees across sessions. What is missing is an explanation of why the same link returned different results on the same date, and a second independent observation on a different date to show the destination is stable rather than merely consistent today.
How to find it out
Open the link from a normal browser on the owner's own network, note the final URL, and re-check monthly. If it ever stops resolving, re-issue it from the Associates dashboard.
Who can do that
Anyone for a single check; the owner for re-issuing the link
Cost of finding out
Two minutes now, two minutes a month

What the site ships meanwhile

P-04 is published as a thermometer with its observed ASIN, and the disagreement is published rather than smoothed over.

D-05Open

What should the site write about next?

What we already have
A set of question-shaped guides, each limited to what the listing titles state. A sitemap and an RSS feed that the build keeps in step with the guide list automatically.
What we do not have
Any evidence about what readers want. There is no Search Console property, no analytics endpoint deployed, and no query data of any kind. Every topic chosen so far was chosen because it followed from the catalogue, not because it was observed.
How to find it out
Verify the site as a property in Search Console and submit the sitemap, then read the queries the site already appears for. Free, and the only source of real demand available before traffic exists. Deploying the first-party counter on the measurement page would add on-site behaviour to the same picture.
Who can do that
Account owner for Search Console verification; an agent can prepare everything else
Cost of finding out
About 30 minutes to verify and submit, then a fortnight of waiting

What the site ships meanwhile

No new guides are commissioned from guesswork. The cadence exists; the topics wait for evidence.

D-06Open

Who is the author, and on what authority does the site speak?

What we already have
No product on this site was handled, cooked with, measured or tested. No byline exists. Every guide states plainly that nothing was tested, and the checker fails the build if a first-person test claim appears.
What we do not have
A named person, and either first-hand use of the products or a stated reason the reader should trust the writing without it. The FTC's Endorsement Guides require an endorsement to reflect the honest opinion of the endorser, which presupposes an endorser.
How to find it out
Two honest routes and no third: buy and use one product and write from that, or name an editor and state exactly what they did and did not do. Inventing a persona is the one option that is not available.
Who can do that
Owner decision — it requires a real name and a real claim
Cost of finding out
One product and an afternoon, or one name and a paragraph

What the site ships meanwhile

Guides are written as checklists of questions, which is what can honestly be produced without access to the products.

D-07Open

Should the two published editions be merged into one?

What we already have
Two editions have been served from this repository: the maintained set generated from src/data, and an older parallel set. Both reached the same five ASINs independently, which is corroboration rather than conflict. The maintained build writes only its own generated files, so it cannot damage the other edition.
What we do not have
A decision. Nothing technical blocks the merge; the editions use different brands and URL layouts, so merging means choosing one and accepting that the other's URLs go away or redirect.
How to find it out
No new information is needed — the reviewer chooses. The reversible half-step available meanwhile is a canonical tag on each duplicate pointing at the maintained equivalent, which consolidates indexing without deleting anything.
Who can do that
Owner decision; an agent can execute either choice
Cost of finding out
Canonical tags about an hour; a full merge about a day including redirects

What the site ships meanwhile

The maintained edition is what the front page, navigation and sitemap point at. Duplicates are proposed as S-15 rather than removed unilaterally.

D-08Open

Should the analytics endpoint be switched on?

What we already have
A client that implements the five specified events, uses no cookie and no browser storage, refuses to run unless an explicit opt-in flag is set, and honours Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control. A collector exists in the repository but is not deployed. GitHub Pages serves static files only and cannot host the collector.
What we do not have
A privacy notice stating what is collected, why, for how long and who to contact. A host for the collector. A decision on retention and on consent.
How to find it out
Write the notice first, then deploy the collector anywhere that accepts a POST, then verify a test event arrives and that no cookie is set. In that order; the notice is the part that cannot be retrofitted honestly.
Who can do that
An agent can write the notice and the collector; the owner must host it and own the retention decision
Cost of finding out
A day, most of it wording

What the site ships meanwhile

Inert. The site measures nothing, and every traffic statement on it says so.

D-09Open

Do the two thermometers belong in this catalogue at all?

What we already have
Three of the five products sit under Home & Kitchen > Small Appliances > Fryers > Air Fryers. Two sit under Kitchen Utensils & Gadgets > Thermometers & Timers > Meat. The two thermometers also differ from each other: one is an instant-read probe, the other a multi-probe wireless unit whose own listing names air fryers among its intended uses.
What we do not have
Nothing factual. The open question is editorial: whether the site is about air fryers, about cooking thermometers, or about both under one kitchen theme.
How to find it out
Search Console queries would answer this better than any opinion, because they show which of the two subjects actually draws readers. Absent that, the owner decides.
Who can do that
Owner decision
Cost of finding out
Zero to decide, a day to restructure

What the site ships meanwhile

Both stay, in separate sections. Neither thermometer is ever described as an air fryer.

D-10Decidable now

How much should be spent on paid acquisition?

What we already have
Program Policies prohibit purchasing, registering or using any Amazon Mark in a search engine, disqualify purchases arriving through a paid search placement, and state that Associates must not use their tags to claim commissions from another programme on the same traffic. The site has no measurement that could attribute a click.
What we do not have
Nothing. The answer follows from the policy and from the absence of measurement.
How to find it out
Nothing to find out.
Who can do that
Answered from official sources
Cost of finding out
Zero

What the site ships meanwhile

Nothing is spent. Recorded as a suggestion so the refusal stays visible after the reasoning is forgotten.

D-11Partly decided

Can the AI-generated imagery stay on the site?

What we already have
Every photograph is generated illustration and every use carries a caption saying it is not a photograph of that product. The review page lists each asset, the intent behind it and every page it appears on.
What we do not have
Any official statement, from Amazon or from the FTC, settling whether labelled AI illustration beside an affiliate link is acceptable. The site's position is a judgement, not a citation: labelled is an editorial convention, unlabelled would be a deception risk.
How to find it out
Replace them with real photographs of the real units. That removes the question instead of answering it, and it needs the products in hand.
Who can do that
Owner — it requires the physical products
Cost of finding out
An afternoon with a phone and a window

What the site ships meanwhile

Illustrations stay, labelled everywhere, with the caption enforced by the build.

D-12Partly decided

What does a commission look like in money and in time?

What we already have
The rate card is behind an Associates sign-in and was not supplied. What is published and was read directly is the payment mechanics: commission income is paid approximately 60 days after the end of the calendar month in which it was earned, and the policy page publishes a Payment Minimum Chart with different minimums per payment method. The figures in that chart are deliberately not restated here — this site's checker fails the build on any currency figure in visible text, and a rule that cannot distinguish a programme minimum from a product price should not be given exceptions. The source link carries the numbers.
What we do not have
The rate for the Home & Kitchen categories these products sit in. No rate is copied from a third-party source anywhere on this site, because a rate with a false provenance is worse than a blank.
How to find it out
Open the rate card in the Associates dashboard and record it with a date. Ten minutes.
Who can do that
Account owner only
Cost of finding out
About 10 minutes

What the site ships meanwhile

The campaign ledger is empty, shows its columns and states the provenance rule. No conversion score and no revenue forecast appears anywhere on the site.

D-13Open

Is the site even indexable where it is published?

What we already have
GitHub Pages serves this repository from a subpath, so every internal link is relative and the checker fails the build on any root-absolute href. A sitemap and a robots.txt pointing at it are generated by the build.
What we do not have
Confirmation that a crawler has fetched the site. Without Search Console verification there is no coverage report and no way to know whether pages are indexed, excluded or simply unfetched.
How to find it out
Verify the property in Search Console, submit the sitemap, and read the coverage report a fortnight later. A site: query in any search engine gives an earlier, rougher signal.
Who can do that
Account owner for verification
Cost of finding out
About 30 minutes, then waiting

What the site ships meanwhile

The sitemap and canonical tags are correct for the subpath. Nothing else can be settled from inside the repository.

The one thing worth doing before anything on this page

D-03 is the whole project in one question: does a click on a supplied link get credited to shopnow047c-20? One click from the owner's own device and one look at the Associates click report answers it, and that report is the only permitted source for the answer. Until then the site can send readers somewhere that pays nothing, and no amount of design or content work changes that.

The second is cheaper still and needs no account at all: verifying the property in Search Console and submitting the sitemap. That is the only route to real query data, and real query data is what turns D-05 from an opinion into a decision.
