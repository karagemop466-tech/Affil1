Five products, seven columns. Two of those columns are the sellers talking about their own products. One is a link that was actually opened. The rest are empty.

No price column · no rating column · no winner column · on purpose

Most comparison tables rank things. This one cannot, because nothing here was tested, and a rank without a test is an opinion dressed as a measurement.

What the table can do is put the sellers' claims next to each other so the shape of the choice is visible. Read the capacity column and you can see a dual-basket machine quoting six quarts across two three-quart baskets sitting beside a single eight-quart basket and a twenty-six-quart oven. Those numbers describe three different kinds of container, which is exactly why a single capacity figure is a poor way to compare.

Reading the table honestly

What the columns are not

01

The capacity column is a claim

Quarts are quoted by the seller and transcribed here unchanged. No basket, cavity or portion was measured. A quart of volume and a quart of usable cooking surface are not the same thing.

02

The attribute column is marketing copy

"Sync finish", "one-touch presets" and "dishwasher-safe" are the seller's phrases. Whether a preset produces good food, or a coating survives your dishwasher, is not something a title can tell you.

03

The empty columns are the honest ones

Price, rating, review count and stock would be the most useful columns on the page. They are absent because Amazon permits them only from data Amazon serves or via its API. The rule, quoted from the source.

A comparison you can actually finish

Measure the space you have. Write down the meal you cook most. Then answer one question for each product: does my usual meal fit in one layer of this thing?
