If you only do five things

An order that holds up

Each step makes the next one cheaper. Skipping step two means steps three to five are measuring a channel that may not pay.

  1. 1

    Tag and check the links

    Without a tracked link, everything downstream measures nothing. It is done and enforced by the build.

    S-14

  2. 2

    Confirm one click in the Associates report

    The only permitted source of truth for attribution. One click answers what no amount of site work can.

    S-01

  3. 3

    Get the site into two indexes

    Search Console and Bing both accept a sitemap and report queries without adding a script to the page.

    S-07, S-13

  4. 4

    Name the editor, honestly

    The cheapest credibility available, provided the biography describes work that actually happened.

    S-03

  5. 5

    Then, and only then, commission more guides

    New topics should come from measured queries, not from another round of guesses.

    S-06, S-11

S-14Implemented

Tag and disclose every outbound link, then enforce it in the build

Amazon's Program Policies require the Associates ID as a URL parameter in each link, and the Commission Income Statement disqualifies purchases that are not correctly tracked because a link was not properly formatted. The build now appends the tag and fails if a link is missing rel=sponsored.

Risk: Low and reversible: the tag is one parameter and the pages are generated.

Source: Associates Program Policies 2(b)

S-15Implemented

Add a category layer so a reader can browse rather than read

A shop needs more than a shortlist and an essay. Two category pages, air fryers and thermometers, give a shopper a place to land from search and give the products a place to sit together.

Risk: Low. The pages are generated from the same product register, so they cannot drift.

Source: USDA FSIS (thermometer category copy)

S-16Implemented

Publish a dated publishing calendar instead of promising one

A cadence that exists only in a chat message is not a cadence. A public page with dates, a status per slot and the rule for filling it makes the promise checkable by the reader, which is the version that survives a busy month.

Risk: Itself: a calendar that goes stale is worse than none. Pair it with the change log in S-10.

Source: Google Search Console

S-17Implemented

Replace the review-page tone on reader pages with an editorial standard

The earlier front page opened with the verification process. A shopper does not want to audit the site before shopping. The verification still exists; it moved to the review queue and the product records, where a reader who wants it can find it.

Risk: Moderate if done carelessly: moving the uncertainty off the page must not remove it from the site. It has not been removed, only relocated and linked.

Source: FTC Endorsement Guides

The one recommendation to read twice

Everything else on this page adds something. S-11 says stop: do not commission more AI-written guides until either a real test or real search data exists. More synthetic prose makes the site bigger, not better, and it raises the chance the whole publication is treated as one undifferentiated block.

The four guides that exist are defensible because they claim little. That is a narrow advantage, and it survives by not being diluted.
