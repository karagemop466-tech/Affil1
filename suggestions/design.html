Copy-ready design prompt

Design Crisp & Counter as an independent kitchen buying publication, not a review farm and not a dashboard. Warm paper background, one dark-green accent, legible serif headlines with generous leading, and food photography or visibly labelled editorial illustrations — never stock filler. The front page leads with the products: a seasonal feature, a short-format shortlist, then the guides. Registers, ledgers and decision tools live one click deep, reachable from the footer.

On mobile, the first screen answers one useful choice: single basket, dual basket, or oven. Every product card carries a concrete cooking scenario (the 6pm frozen-chips panic, the six-person Sunday dinner), one honest trade-off, and one thing to check next — never a score, a rank, or a superlative. Make the comparison table readable by keyboard and at 360 px. Give every guide a consistent byline slot, a sources block and related reading.

Anti-slop rules, non-negotiable: no first-person testing claims; no superlatives, award language or ranking claims; no prices, ratings or review counts; no hollow meta (“in this article”, “look no further”); no two sentences with the same rhythm in a row; every factual product claim carries a source or is left blank and flagged. The disclosure strip goes under the masthead on every page and again in the footer, carrying “As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.” verbatim. Do not show a tracked shopping CTA until the destination and attribution are verified. The ten numbered house-style rules below are the gate; this paragraph is their summary.

What this bank is not

None of these titles is a claim about search volume, popularity or trend data — no keyword tool was consulted. They are shaped like the questions buyers ask because that is the only defensible way to guess before measurement exists. The gate is S-23: Search Console (S-03) confirms which questions are real, then those topics get written. T-07 is on hold until a citable source exists; a guessed safety answer is the one thing this site cannot ship.

ORGANIC LOOP

How traffic is earned here

Organic traffic is earned by one indexable site that answers real questions and can prove it. The loop is publish → sitemap and RSS → internal link → measure queries → choose the next topic from the measured ones. Nothing in the loop guesses demand.

Already in place

  • One canonical site — no competing edition is indexable (archive/ is noindex; robots.txt blocks it).
  • Sitemap and RSS feed regenerated from the same data as the pages, so no published guide is missing from either.
  • Canonical URLs, Open Graph images and Article/FAQPage/BreadcrumbList structured data on generated pages.
  • Question-shaped guides in deliberate clusters, each linking to its cluster peers and to the product pages.
  • A disclosure strip and a per-link sponsored mark on every page, so a search engine never has to guess the relationship.

Missing, and gated behind a decision

  • Search Console property and sitemap submission (S-03) — needs the owner's Google account. Until it exists, every topic choice is an editorial hypothesis (Q-07).
  • 28-day query review against the previous window (S-07 gate) — needs the report, not instinct.
  • A root domain instead of the repository subpath (S-11) — a brand and budget decision (Q-17).
  • Any paid acquisition — refused as policy (S-21), recorded so it is not rediscovered as an idea.

Official reading: Google Search Central: people-first content · Google Search Central: qualify outbound links (rel="sponsored") · Google Search Console Performance report

A cadence that can respond to results

  1. Weekly: Check indexing and query/page patterns in the verified Search Console property. Log the date range and missing data. The first themes are small kitchens, meal prep, holiday sides, and food thermometers, not proven search trends.
  2. Every other week: Publish one distinct question or improve an existing guide. Update an existing page rather than creating a near-duplicate. The build adds each accepted guide to the sitemap and RSS feed.
  3. Every 28 days: Review query and landing-page data against the previous comparable window. Keep on-site outbound counts separate from Associates orders. If the data are sparse, improve the guide or keep collecting; don’t estimate demand, commissions, or conversion.
