Q-28Open Blocks work

Which first-party collector is canonical, and does the owner accept running it?

What we have

Q-29Open

May a guide that ends in a buy button quote government food-safety limits, and who owns that risk?

What we have
Several guides quote USDA FSIS fact sheets sentence for sentence - safe minimum internal temperatures, probe placement for thin foods, the ice-water and boiling-water calibration procedures, the two-hour limit for perishable food left out, the 3-to-4-day storage window - each with a link to the page quoted and a closing note stating that nothing on this site was tested. The quotations were read from the .gov pages on 2026-11-20, not from a search result or a snippet.
What is missing
The owner's view on whether health-adjacent guidance next to affiliate links is wanted at all, and whether it should carry a review step. There is no food-safety reviewer here, so the only control today is that the site quotes rather than advises.

What would close it

Either accept the rule as written (quote the agency, link the page, add no interpretation) or restrict guides to non-health questions. Both are defensible; the choice decides which topics in the bank can ever ship. If a reviewer is wanted, name them before the next such article, not after.

Q-30Open

What happens to a quoted guide when the official page it cites changes?

What we have
Every source in the register carries a retrieval date and, where the publisher states one, a page date - one fact sheet used by the leftovers guide says on its face that it was last updated Jul 31, 2020. The weekly workflow re-runs the gate against a live snapshot, but it checks links, disclosure and the price ban; it does not check whether a cited page still says the quoted thing.
What is missing
A refresh rule: which guides are re-read against their sources, how often, and what happens when a quote no longer appears (fix the quote, or date-stamp it and move the page to /review/). Without it, a correct article becomes a stale one silently.

What would close it

Add a source_review_due date to each guide record and extend the weekly audit to fetch each cited .gov page and diff the quoted sentence; anything that no longer matches is flagged automatically rather than edited in place. Cheaper to build now, at 16 guides, than after 60.

Q-05Answered

Is the disclosure in the right place, and does it say enough?

What we have
A disclosure strip sits under the masthead on every page, above the content, and is repeated in the footer. It states the Associates relationship in the sentence the Operating Agreement requires.
What is missing
Nothing structural. The FTC frames placement as a judgement about prominence, and says the closer the disclosure is to the recommendation, the better (SRC-03).

Answer

Above the fold on every page, repeated in the footer, and reinforced with a 'Paid links' label next to it. The FTC guidance explicitly treats a bare 'affiliate link' label as possibly insufficient, which is why the strip carries a full sentence as well as the label.

Source: SRC-01, SRC-03
