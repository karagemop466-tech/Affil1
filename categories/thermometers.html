Meat thermometers

Two thermometers, and the numbers worth aiming for

An instant-read probe you move from piece to piece, and a leave-in set that reports while you do something else. Both answer the same question.

2 products · nothing tested · no prices shown

The question a thermometer answers is not whether the food looks done. It is what the internal temperature is, measured at the thickest part, away from bone and fat. The USDA publishes the minimums, and they are the only numbers on this site quoted as fact rather than as a seller's claim.

The choice between these two is about how you cook. A probe you move is right when you are standing at the pan. A set that stays in is right when the food goes into an oven, a smoker or an air fryer and you would rather not open the door to check.

What actually decides it

Will you be standing there?
If yes, an instant-read is faster to use and cheaper to own.
Does the food cook behind a closed door?
Then a leave-in probe avoids opening the oven and losing heat, which is the main practical argument for it.
Do you need more than one reading at once?
A joint and a bird, or four steaks, need more than one probe or more than one check.

What to aim for

Measured with a food thermometer at the thickest part, before the food leaves the heat. The USDA calls these minimums; you may prefer higher.
