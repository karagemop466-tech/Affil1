How traffic actually arrives

Twelve guides.
Three clusters. One cadence.

A shop earns readers by answering questions better than the ten listicles above it. This plan says which questions, in what order, with what evidence, and what each page must contain before it is allowed out. Working titles, not predictions: nothing here claims a search volume, and no ranking is promised.

The clustersFindability checklist

The rule this plan is built on

Publish less, prove more

Google calls a page with copied descriptions and no added value a thin affiliate page, and it names what fixes it: extra price context, original reviews, real testing, clear category navigation, honest comparisons. Two of those five are unavailable to us right now, so this plan leans on the three that are.

Source: Spam policies for Google web search, read 2026-11-20. See G-01, G-02.

Beat, not batch

Three clusters, twelve guides

Every guide sits in one cluster, links two siblings and its cluster index, and answers a question a person actually asks standing in a kitchen. Four of the twelve already exist as drafts in this repository.

Cluster 1 Choosing a format

01

Exists as a draft · 6 min · air fryers

Basket or oven-style air fryer: what should you actually compare?

Batch size against footprint, and the one question that settles it: do you want to replace the oven or skip using it? Ends with a two-minute self-assessment, not a verdict.

Evidence still needed: none for the framing; the product links stay unmonetised until Q-03.

02

New · 7 min · dual-zone

Two baskets, one dinner: what a dual-zone fryer fixes, and what it doesn't

Written around the failure mode nobody mentions: sync finish helps when both baskets run the same food on different timers, and does nothing when one side needs a different temperature curve. Uses the Chefman 6 Qt listing copy as the worked example, attributed and dated.

Do not publish before P-01 is confirmed in the enrolled workflow.

03

New · 5 min · capacity

The quart question: how much counter you really have

A tape-measure walk-through that turns "8 qt" into centimetres, wall clearance and the cabinet that won't shut. Two worked examples from the shelf, both from listing dimensions with a capture date.

No capacity claims of our own — the page compares numbers, never cooking performance.

04

New · 6 min · overlap

Air fryer or toaster oven: the honest overlap

Where the two machines are the same machine, where they aren't, and the sentence to write if you only ever cook for one: probably nothing, and that's fine. This is the guide that earns trust, because it talks people out of a purchase.

Cluster 2 Living with it

05

Exists as a draft · 5 min · capacity

A no-hype checklist for comparing air-fryer capacity

Servings are a marketing noun. The checklist asks for basket floor area, tray width and the height of a whole chicken lying down, and it explains why "quarts" measures volume and not food.

06

Exists as a draft · 4 min · cleanup

How to assess cleanup and counter-space trade-offs

Wash-up minutes are the real cost of a countertop appliance. A structured way to judge coatings, baskets that fit a dishwasher rack, and the grease ring behind a unit that never moves.

07

New · 4 min · ventilation

Eleven centimetres: the clearance rule your countertop breaks

Exhaust placement, wall gaps, and the under-cabinet problem. Deliberately written as a measuring exercise, because we have not put a unit in a kitchen yet and saying so is the point.

08

New · 3 min · noise

The 9 p.m. test: what these things actually sound like

The one honest angle left in this niche — nobody writes about the fan at a decibel level, because nobody has measured. Until we have, this page is a protocol and a question, not a result. That is stated in the first line.

Worth holding until Q-13 is answered, or publishing explicitly as a method.

Cadence, and how it stops

One guide a fortnight — with a brake

A rhythm is only useful if it can be interrupted by evidence. This one is defined with its own kill conditions.

Week 0 · gateDo not write yet. Close Q-01, Q-02, Q-03. A guide aimed at an unconfirmed product is a rewrite.
Week 1 · draftOne guide from the cluster with the most holes, written against a checklist: answer in the first 100 words, sources dated, nothing asserted about performance.
Week 2 · publishAdd to the canonical sitemap and the single feed, link two siblings, verify in Search Console's URL inspection. One commit, dated provenance in the data file.
Monthly · readQueries and impressions from Search Console decide the next title. If a page gets impressions and no clicks, the title is wrong; fix that before writing another.
Quarterly · pruneRetire or merge anything with zero impressions for two quarters. Deletion is an editorial act, and it is the one most affiliate sites never perform.

Stop conditions

Two consecutive guides with zero impressions after eight weeks → the niche or the titles are wrong, not the volume. Amazon disapproves the site, or the tag cannot be confirmed → stop adding links; keep the writing.

Never conditions

No mass generation. No spun variants of one page across clusters. No publishing a price, a rating or a rank without a dated, permitted source. No "we tested" until someone has tested.

Why twelve, not two hundred

Twelve pages in three clusters is a beat a reader can see from the navigation. Two hundred pages in one cluster is what the thin-affiliate policy was written to describe — and with no API access, the two hundred would all be guessing.

The slop test

Six checks before any draft ships

Each is mechanical enough to automate and editorial enough to matter. A draft that fails one is rewritten, not published with a hedge.

1 · Could this sentence be about any product?

If yes, delete it. Every paragraph in a guide must survive being read next to the specific listing it came from.

2 · Is there a number with a date?

Any figure carries the day it was read and the page it came from. Undated numbers are how a spec sheet turns into a lie after a quarter.

3 · Does it say who wrote it?

A byline, a person, and a route to correct them. Anonymous advice is the loudest possible tell.

4 · Does it ever argue against the purchase?

A page with no reasons to walk away is an advertisement wearing a question mark.

5 · Would it still be true tomorrow?

If it depends on price, stock, rank or a promotion, it is not publishable here — see the price rule in the compliance gate.

6 · Read it aloud once

Cut every sentence a person would not say to a friend standing at a counter. This one cannot be automated, which is exactly why it works.
