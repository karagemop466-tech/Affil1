Executive summary

What was built, what was verified, what could not be, and the three decisions that would move this furthest. Written for someone who has five minutes.

What this is

A publisher-style shop for four kitchen appliances, built as a static site and deployed to GitHub Pages, with the verification layer published alongside it instead of hidden in a spreadsheet. The site has a front page, a shop index, four product pages, four buying guides, an about page, and eight record pages covering sources, intent, compliance, measurement, the ledger, open questions, suggestions and flagged items.

What was verified, line by line

Twenty-four claims were checked against official pages during a single pass on 2026-11-20, and a second integration pass the same day re-read sixteen of them verbatim from the official pages (the row-level spot-check notes are on the source register). They come from Amazon Associates pages (the Operating Agreement, updated October 15, 2025, and the Program Policies, updated April 14, 2026), FTC guidance, Google's sitemap documentation, GitHub's Pages documentation, and Amazon's developer documentation. Three are marked unverified with the value column left empty rather than guessed: the commission rate schedule sits behind an Associates sign-in, the Seller Central ASIN-definition page errors without sign-in so the 10-character shape stays an observation, and the supplied short-link codes' shape claim is not restated without a reachable source.

The single most load-bearing finding for the build: Amazon's Operating Agreement requires the disclosure sentence in specific words, and the FTC's guidance says a disclosure at the bottom of a page or on an about page is likely to be missed. Those two together are why the disclosure strip sits directly under the masthead on every page, in the required wording, and again in the footer.

The three decisions that matter most

1. Confirm the intended ASIN for each link

Four products are already published on observed destinations. Stating the intended ASIN converts four unconfirmed rows into confirmed ones, and catches any variant mismatch — two of the listings offer multiple sizes or probe counts.

2. Decide whether to keep the short-link format

Whether link.amazon links count as compliant Special Links is genuinely open. Published outbound links use the canonical tagged url instead, which sidesteps the question rather than answering it. If the short form is confirmed fine, nothing changes; if it is not, nothing published is at risk.

3. Name an author and connect Search Console

A named author is what makes the guides claimable as a publication rather than a comparison sheet. Search Console is what makes guide five a measured decision instead of a hunch. Both are small inputs with outsized effects.

What was deliberately not done

No price is published, because Amazon permits prices only when Amazon serves them or when they come from an approved API, and a copied price is wrong within the hour. No star rating or review quote is published, for the same class of reason. No product is claimed to have been tested, because none was — and the FTC is direct on that point. No revenue is forecast, and the campaign ledger is empty rather than illustrative. No paid traffic is recommended, because two separate rules bear on it.

The honest weakness

The site is strongest on what it can prove and weakest on the thing every affiliate site needs most: why these five products, chosen by whom, on what basis. That is not a design failure to be hidden; it is the largest single input missing, and it is first on the flagged list for that reason. A shop with a named buyer and a stated criterion would be a materially different proposition, and none of that can be invented without lying about it.

Where to look next

Flagged for review for the eight open items, open questions for what blocks what, suggestions for the paths not taken, and the source register for a link behind every claim.
