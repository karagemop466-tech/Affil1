Checklist

A no-hype checklist for comparing air fryer capacity

Quart numbers are the least comparable spec on the box. Here is what to check instead, in the order that actually changes a buying decision.

By the shopnow047c-20 desk 5 min read Reviewed 2026-11-20 No hands-on testing claimed

Check whether the number is total capacity or usable capacity

"6 quarts" can mean one 6-quart basket or two 3-quart baskets. Those are materially different appliances. Two 3-quart baskets cannot hold one large item; one 6-quart basket cannot hold two foods at different temperatures.

Before comparing any two units, write down the shape, not just the number: how many compartments, and how big is the largest single compartment.

Check the largest single item you can fit

Capacity tells you volume. It does not tell you whether a whole chicken, a pizza, or a nine-inch pan will physically go in. Look for the internal dimensions of the largest compartment, and check the shape of the basket floor.

If you plan to cook whole poultry, this is the check that decides the purchase. A thermometer will tell you when it is done; nothing will make it fit.

Check the temperature range against your recipes

Some units stop at 400°F; some reach 450°F. Higher ceilings matter for crisp finishes and for anything you would normally broil. Lower floors matter for dehydrating and proofing.

Match the range to two or three recipes you actually cook, not to the maximum the market offers.

Check what fraction of the basket you will realistically fill

Air fryers need room for air to move. Overfilling produces steamed food, not crisp food. A basket rated for four portions usually does two well if the food needs to be spread in one layer.

Halve the marketing portion count, then check whether the unit is still big enough. It usually is.

Check the counter and cupboard maths before the capacity maths

An air fryer that does not fit on the counter gets stored in a cupboard, and an air fryer in a cupboard gets used once a month. Measure the standing footprint and the height with the handle out.

If it only fits in a cupboard, be honest that you are buying a sometimes-appliance.

The short version

Write down three things before you compare anything: largest single item, how many foods at once, and counter space available. Then ignore every quart number that does not answer those three.
