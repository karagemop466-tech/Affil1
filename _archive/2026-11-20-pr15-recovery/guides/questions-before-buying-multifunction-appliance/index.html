Before you buy

What should you ask before buying a multi-function kitchen appliance?

Multi-function appliances promise everything and are judged on the one thing you bought them for. These questions keep the promise honest.

By the shopnow047c-20 desk 6 min read Reviewed 2026-11-20 No hands-on testing claimed

"Which function am I actually buying this for?"

A 24-preset oven is bought for one or two of those presets. Name yours out loud before you buy. If you cannot name two, the appliance is a gadget purchase rather than a replacement purchase, which is fine, but price it that way.

The functions you will not use still take up counter space and still need cleaning.

"What does this replace?"

The strongest case for a multi-function appliance is that it removes two others from the counter. A French-door air fryer oven that replaces a toaster oven is a net gain. The same unit added alongside a toaster oven is a net loss.

Count what leaves the counter, not what the box claims it can do.

"Does it depend on an app, and what happens when the app breaks?"

Connected appliances are only as reliable as their software. Ask whether the core function still works without the phone, and whether the app is required for setup.

For a thermometer, the app is the product. For an oven, the app should be a bonus. If a device's primary function lives in an app you do not control, treat that as durability risk.

"What is the consumable or accessory cost?"

Probes break, liners get used up, filters need replacing. Ask what is consumable and roughly how often. An appliance with an ongoing accessory cost is not the same purchase as a sealed unit.

Check whether replacement parts are sold by the manufacturer rather than only as third-party bundles.

"Where does it physically live?"

Multi-function appliances are, almost by definition, large. If the answer is "in the cupboard, and I will get it out", be sceptical. Heavy appliances that live in cupboards get used rarely.

Decide the permanent home before you buy, including clearance for doors and handles.

"What would make me return it?"

Name the failure in advance: too loud, too slow to preheat, impossible to clean, does not fit. Knowing the return trigger makes you evaluate the appliance in the first week instead of discovering the flaw in month six.

Check the return window before purchase, and use the appliance properly inside it.

The short version

Name the function you are buying, the appliance it replaces, and the failure that would make you return it. If you cannot answer all three, wait a week.
