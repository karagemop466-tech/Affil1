The dial is not the temperature: what a thermometer actually tells you

Scheduled for the week of 4 December 2026. A short guide to reading an instant-read against USDA's safe minimum internal temperatures.

What this guide will cover

Oven dials and air-fryer presets set an air temperature, not a food temperature. This guide will explain the difference, how to take a reading properly, and where to find the official minimum internal temperatures rather than a blog's version of them.

The one sourced fact already available

USDA's Food Safety and Inspection Service publishes a safe minimum internal temperature chart: poultry to 165°F, ground meats to 160°F, whole cuts of beef, pork, veal and lamb to 145°F with a three-minute rest, and leftovers to 165°F. That chart is the authority this guide will cite, and it is linked in the sources on this page.

The rest of the guide waits on one thing: a checked reading from the thermometers in this shop. Until we have that, the page will not claim either one is accurate.

What this guide will not do

It will not tell you which machine is best. Nobody here has tested one, and the FTC's guidance is clear that an endorsement has to reflect an honest experience. What the guide does is narrow the field using the questions a listing will not answer for you.
