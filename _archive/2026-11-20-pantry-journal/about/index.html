About

Who writes this, and what they will not do

A short statement of method, so you can decide how much to trust the rest.

The Pantry Journal covers countertop cooking appliances: air fryers in the two main shapes, and the thermometers that make cooking with them predictable. It is a small shop on purpose. The alternative — hundreds of near-identical pages — is how buying advice stops being advice.

Everything factual here comes from one of three places: the seller's own listing title, an official policy or guidance document with a link, or a dated observation recorded by the person who built this site. Where none of those applies, the page asks a question instead of giving an answer.

There are no prices, star ratings, review counts or stock figures anywhere, and that is a rule rather than an accident. Amazon restricts how associates display that data; without a compliant feed the honest option is to send you to the listing and let it tell you.

The disclosure, in full

As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.

As an Amazon Associate, The Pantry Journal earns from qualifying purchases. Links marked with a small arrow go to Amazon. If you buy through one, this site may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. The full sentence sits in the strip under the masthead on every page of this site, and again in the footer. Amazon’s Operating Agreement, section 5, requires the statement to be clearly and prominently displayed; the FTC’s guidance asks that it be close enough to the recommendation to be weighed at the moment of reading. A footer alone fails that test on a long page, so it is above the first link.

Sources: Associates Program Operating Agreement, section 5 · FTC Endorsement Guides: What People Are Asking. Both read 20 November 2026.

On the AI part

An honest note on the AI part: the drafting, the page templates and the source checking were done with AI assistance. The sourcing standard was not. If you find a claim that is not traceable to a listing title, a policy document or a dated observation, that is a defect — report it on the Suggestions page and it will be corrected.

What this shop will not do

  • It will not publish a price, a star rating or a stock figure, because it cannot source them lawfully.
  • It will not call a machine the best, because nobody here has tested one.
  • It will not use urgency — no countdowns, no “today only”, no deal badges that expire silently.
  • It will not repeat a manufacturer’s performance claim as though it were a fact.
  • It will not track you. No cookie, no pixel, no third-party script.
  • It will not hide a gap. Every unresolved item is on the Flagged for review page.

How to challenge something

Two routes, both public. The Suggestions page is a review queue where a proposed change can be compared against the alternatives before anything ships. The Decisions page lists what could not be answered and what would answer it. Neither page is decoration: they are how this shop stays honest as it grows.
