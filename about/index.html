Crisp & Counter is a small buyer’s guide to five kitchen products. It is written and maintained by one editor, it takes money only from affiliate links, and it does not sell placement. This page states the rules so you can hold the site to them.

Five products · six guides · no prices shown · nothing sponsored beyond the links

What this site is for

You are standing in a kitchen with a tape measure and an appliance to choose. The job here is to help you decide in ten minutes and buy once, not to talk you into the biggest one. That makes the guides the most useful part of the site, and the product pages the shortest route from a guide to a listing.

It is not a review site. Nothing here was bought, cooked with, measured or compared by testing. You will see that stated on every product page rather than in a footnote, because a page that implies testing it did not do is the one thing that would make everything else here worthless.

How the writing works

Every guide is written to a fixed set of rules, and the build fails if any page breaks one:

  1. Product facts come from the listing, and are labelled as such. If a page says a machine has two 3-quart baskets, that is the seller’s wording, transcribed once and marked “seller-stated” wherever it appears.
  2. No prices, star ratings, review counts or stock status. Amazon permits those only when Amazon serves the data or they arrive through its API. Neither is connected, so they are absent site-wide.
  3. No test claims, no winners, no rankings. A machine-checked rule, not an aspiration.
  4. Independent numbers are cited to the agency that published them. The only such numbers on the site are food-safety temperatures from the USDA, and each is linked.
  5. Nothing is generated and published unreviewed. Drafts may be drafted with software; the claim, the source and the disclosure placement are checked by a person before a page ships.

Where a fact was needed and could not be sourced, the page says so. That is why you will see empty cells: an empty cell is a question mark, and a filled-in cell nobody verified is a lie with a font.

Why so much is blank

Amazon’s Associates Program Policies, section 2(b), say a site may show prices and availability only if Amazon serves the link carrying that data, or if the site obtains it through the Creators API or Product Advertising API. This build has access to neither, so price, star rating, review count and stock status are absent everywhere — not because they are hard to copy, but because copying them is the specific thing that clause forbids.

The same discipline applies to fields nobody regulates. Dimensions, wattage and warranty terms were not confirmed from an official specification sheet in this build, so they are empty too. An empty cell is a question mark. A filled-in cell that nobody verified is a lie with a font.

How the links work

Every link from this site to an Amazon product carries three things: the Associates tracking tag as a URL parameter, rel="sponsored nofollow noopener", and a visible note saying it is a paid link. Google documents sponsored as the marker for paid links, and Amazon’s Program Policies require the tag in the URL of each link.

The links are built once, in one function, so a page cannot be added with an unmarked or untagged link without failing the build. That is deliberate: it is the mistake that would cost the site both its credibility and its account.

The tracking ID supplied for this site is shopnow047c-20. It is applied to every link. Whether a real click is credited to it has not yet been observed in the Associates report, which is recorded openly as FLG-002 rather than claimed as working.

The disclosure

As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.

That sentence is required by the Associates Program Operating Agreement and appears above the masthead on every page, again in the footer, and once more beside every buy button. The FTC’s guidance is that a disclosure must be hard to miss and close to the recommendation; a footer-only notice fails both tests, which is why this site does not rely on one.

The photographs are illustrations

Every image here is an AI-generated illustration, labelled on the page where it appears and never presented as a photograph of the product being sold. Every asset is listed with the rule it follows and what the site declined to generate.

Who wrote this

One editor, no byline yet. Naming a person who did not do the work would be worse than the blank, so the blank is recorded as FLG-006 and publishing a true short biography is the first item in the suggestion queue. If you want to know what this site is better at than a large review outlet, the honest answer is: it is more careful about what it does not know.

Privacy

No cookies, no third-party scripts, no CDN, no embedded media, no fonts fetched from anyone else’s server. The analytics client is written and switched off. There is no form and no mailing list, so the site holds no personal data at all. Read the privacy page.
