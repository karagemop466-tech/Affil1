Your first air fryer

You have never owned one and do not want a cupboard ornament.

One basket, and nothing else.

A single drawer has one part to wash, one control to learn and no second zone to fill. The usual first-owner mistake is buying the most complicated shape and discovering the cleaning, not the cooking, decides whether it gets used again.

Measure this
The footprint of the spot it will live in, the height under the cabinet above it, and the width of the biggest batch you actually cook. Then compare that batch with the basket dimensions in the manual, not with the quart figure.
What settles it
The cleanup guide, because it is the part that decides the answer.

Dinner and a side, at once

One appliance, two things, and you would rather they landed together.

Two independent baskets.

Two drawers only help if they run independently and can be told to finish at the same time. If both drawers share one setting, you have bought one basket with a divider and a bigger washing-up bill.

Measure this
Each basket separately. Six quarts as two three-quart drawers is not the same as six quarts in one drawer, and it is certainly not comparable with an eight-quart single basket.
What settles it
The dual-zone guide, which asks how often you really cook two things.

A small counter

The kitchen is fine. The worktop is the problem.

Whatever leaves you somewhere to chop.

Counter space is a zero-sum budget. A machine that needs its drawer pulled out or its doors swung open also owns the space in front of it, which is usually the space you stand in.

Measure this
Clear depth with the drawer open or the doors down, plus the socket distance, because a short cord quietly relocates the appliance for you.
What settles it
The space audit, which is a tape measure and ten minutes.

Replacing a toaster oven

You want one box that toasts, bakes, reheats and air-fries.

An oven-style cavity with a door.

A shallow cavity takes a tray, a rack, a pan or a plate. A deep drawer mostly takes loose food. If you want to put a pan in it, the shape decides before the brand does.

Measure this
The tray you already own, the usable rack dimensions from the manual, and the swing of the doors. Then count the accessories, because each one is a separate thing to wash and store.
What settles it
The multi-function questions, written for exactly this decision.

Stop guessing whether it is done

The outside looks right and you do not trust the middle.

A thermometer, and it is the cheapest thing on this page.

An air fryer can brown the outside while the centre is under. The USDA's own guidance is blunt that colour and firmness are not indicators that food is safe, and that a food thermometer is the reliable check — at any price point.

Measure this
Nothing. This one is decided by whether you want a reading or a leave-in probe.
What settles it
The pairing guide, which explains when a leave-in probe beats a quick read.
